✖️ Section 4 — Times Tables & Multiples

⚠️ Q16 — Multiples of 4 in a list

From the list 8, 14, 4, 6, 36, 18 — circle ALL multiples of 4.
The multiples of 4 are: 8 (4×2), 4 (4×1), and 36 (4×9). Not 14, 6 or 18.

✗ 14, 6, 18 are NOT multiples of 4
None of these divide evenly by 4.
✓ 4, 8, 36 ARE multiples of 4 ✅
4÷4=1, 8÷4=2, 36÷4=9 — all divide exactly.

⭐ A multiple of a number is what you get when you multiply it: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40…

🔑 Quick test: divide the number by 4 — if there is no remainder, it IS a multiple. If there is a remainder, it is NOT.

e.g. 36 ÷ 4 = 9 (no remainder) → ✅ multiple · 14 ÷ 4 = 3 remainder 2 → ❌ not a multiple

📐 Section 6 — Area & Perimeter

⚠️ Q12 — Draw a rectangle with area 12 square centimetres

Area = 12 sq cm means the rectangle must contain exactly 12 grid squares.
Possible rectangles: 3 × 4, 2 × 6, or 1 × 12.

📐 Area = length × width (count the squares inside) · measured in square units (sq cm)

📐 Perimeter = add up ALL the sides · measured in cm (or m, km)

Example: rectangle 3 × 4 → Area = 3 × 4 = 12 sq cm · Perimeter = 3+4+3+4 = 14 cm
