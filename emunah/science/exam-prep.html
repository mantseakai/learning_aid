🦕 Section 4 — Types of Fossils

⚠️ Q3b Mistake — "Cast fossil" is a type of fossil, NOT an organism!

The question asked: "What type of organism made this fossil?" (showing a fish fossil)
You wrote "cast fossil" ❌ — that is the type of fossil, not the organism (living thing) that made it!
The correct answer is simply "fish" — because the fossil shows the shape of a fish.

✗ "cast fossil" ❌
"Cast fossil" describes HOW the fossil was made — it is not a living thing.
✓ "fish" ✅
The organism (living thing) is a FISH. The type of fossil is separate.

🦕 A fossil is the preserved remains or impression of a living thing (plant or animal) that lived millions of years ago, usually found in rock or mud.

There are two separate things to know: (1) the TYPE of fossil (how it was preserved), and (2) the ORGANISM (what living thing made it — fish, dinosaur, plant, etc.)

🐾
Trace Fossil
Preserved evidence of an animal's activity — footprints, burrows, bite marks. The animal itself is NOT preserved.
🌕 Astronaut footprint on Moon = trace fossil
🐟
Body Fossil
The actual body parts of an organism are preserved — bones, shells, teeth, leaves, wood.
🐟 Fish skeleton preserved in rock
🪨
Mould Fossil
The organism dissolves and leaves a hollow impression (like a mould) in the rock. The shape remains but not the animal.
Shape left in rock after shell dissolves
⚗️
Cast Fossil
Minerals fill a mould fossil to create a solid copy of the original organism. Like making a plaster cast.
Solid rock copy of a shell or bone

💡 Key trick: When a question asks "what type of organism?" it wants the LIVING THING — e.g. fish, dinosaur, plant, insect, bird.
When a question asks "what type of fossil?" it wants the FOSSIL TYPE — trace, body, mould, cast.

🌍 Section 6 — Gravity & Forces

🌍 Gravity is a force that pulls objects towards the centre of the Earth. It is why things fall down when you drop them.

⚡ Force is a push or a pull. Force is measured in newtons (N) using a forcemeter (also called a newton meter or spring balance).

🔥 Friction is a force that acts between two surfaces in contact. It slows things down or stops them from moving. Rough surfaces have MORE friction. Smooth surfaces have LESS friction.

🌍
Gravity
Pulls everything towards Earth's centre. This is why things fall down.
Unit: Newtons (N)
🔥
Friction
A force between two touching surfaces. Opposes movement. Rough = more friction.
